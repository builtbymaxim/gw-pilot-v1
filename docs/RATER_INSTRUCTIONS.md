# Greenwashing Language Rating — Instructions

You will read one sentence from an asset manager's annual report and answer 3 questions (1–5 scale):

1) **Vagueness** — How vague is the claim?
   - 1 = very specific (clear numbers, timeframes, baselines)
   - 3 = moderately vague (some detail but incomplete)
   - 5 = very vague (generic, aspirational, no verifiable detail)

2) **Measurability** — How easy is it to verify the claim?
   - 1 = not measurable (no quantity, unit, baseline, or date)
   - 3 = partially measurable (one or two elements present)
   - 5 = clearly measurable (quantity + unit and/or baseline + date)

3) **Overall greenwashing risk**
   - 1 = low risk (specific & measurable)
   - 3 = medium risk (mixed signals)
   - 5 = high risk (vague & non-measurable)

**General rules**
- Rate the sentence as written (no external context).
- Ignore marketing tone; focus on factual clarity and verifiability.
- If the sentence has both specific and vague parts, weigh both; avoid defaulting to 3.
- Units and dates increase measurability; hedges (“may”, “aim to”) increase vagueness.

**Examples (orientation)**
- “Reduce Scope 1–2 emissions **50% by 2030** vs **2019 baseline**.” → Vagueness=1, Measurability=5, Overall risk=1–2
- “We **aim to** improve our environmental footprint.” → Vagueness=5, Measurability=1, Overall risk=4–5

Thank you for your careful judgments.
